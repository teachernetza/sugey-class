Module 1: The Basics with "Very"

"Very" significa "muy". Es el intensificador más común y versátil. Se puede usar de dos formas principales: con el adjetivo solo, o con el adjetivo seguido de un sustantivo.

1. Very + Adjetivo solo

  • The hotel was very clean.
  • The flight is very long.
  • The guide was very helpful.

2. A/An + Very + Adjetivo + Sustantivo

  • It was a very clean hotel.
  • It is a very long flight.
  • He was a very helpful guide.

Mini-Reading in Context

"My trip to London was very nice. The museums were very interesting, but the tickets were very expensive. We stayed in a very small room, but it was very cozy."

Pronunciation Shadowing

"It is a very picturesque town."

Module 2: The Emotional "So"

"So" también significa "muy" o "tan", pero expresa más emoción, asombro o énfasis. La regla de oro es: El adjetivo debe estar SOLO. NO puedes poner un sustantivo (una cosa/persona) después del adjetivo cuando usas "so".

So + Adjetivo (¡Y punto!)

  • The beach is so beautiful. (Correcto, termina en el adjetivo)
  • The local food was so delicious!
  • That museum is so fascinating.
  • It is a so beautiful beach. (INCORRECTO. Hay un sustantivo "beach" después)

Mini-Reading in Context

"We arrived in Cancun yesterday. The water is so clear and the sand is so white! I love it here because the local people are so friendly, and the food is so delicious."

Pronunciation Shadowing

"The sunset at the beach was so beautiful."

Module 3: Deep Dive into "Such"

¿Por qué existe "Such"? Cuando quieres expresar la emoción de "So" (¡Qué increíble! / ¡Es tan...! ), pero tu oración termina con un sustantivo (una cosa, lugar o persona), el inglés te obliga a cambiar "So" por "Such".

Regla 1: Singular

Such a / an + Adj + Sustantivo

Si estás hablando de una sola cosa (a city, a hotel, a trip), debes incluir "a" o "an" después de such.

  • Paris is such a beautiful city.
  • It was such an expensive hotel.
  • This is such a lively neighborhood.
Regla 2: Plurales o Incontables

Such + Adj + Sustantivo

Si hablas de varias cosas (people, places) o cosas que no se cuentan (weather, food, luggage), elimina el "a/an".

  • They are such friendly people.
  • We had such good weather.
  • There were such beautiful views.

Mini-Reading in Context

"I highly recommend visiting Japan. It is such a fascinating country. The locals are such polite people. We had such delicious food every day. Truly, it was such an unforgettable trip."

Pronunciation Shadowing

"It is such a picturesque town."
"They are such friendly people."
